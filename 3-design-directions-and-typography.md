# 3. Design directions and typography

The client asked to see a range, from classic to modern, and said they are open to something bolder than they would choose themselves. So make the three directions genuinely different. Three versions of the same idea is a wasted week.

All three keep the logo, the blue, the gold and the light base. What changes is typography, spacing, and how the blue and gold are used.

## Colour, all three directions

Base is white or a very soft off white. Blue carries structure: frames, borders, section dividers, navigation. Gold is accent only, used sparingly so it still means something. Dark blue blocks are allowed as bands, never behind long text.

Put every colour in a CSS variable at the top of the file. The new gold Pantone is still coming from the client and we will swap it once.

## Direction A: Classic

Traditional European stud feel. Restrained, timeless, close to what they know.

- Headings: Cormorant Garamond or EB Garamond
- Body: Inter or Source Sans 3
- Generous line height, wide margins, plenty of white space
- Thin blue rules between sections, gold only on hover states and small labels
- Cards with a fine border rather than a shadow

## Direction B: Modern editorial

Magazine feel. More contrast between headline and body, bigger type, more confident layout.

- Headings: Instrument Serif or Fraunces
- Body: Manrope or Geist
- Large display headings, tight tracking, asymmetric layouts
- Full bleed images, text overlapping image edges
- Gold as a thin underline or marker on key words

## Direction C: Contemporary refined

The most modern of the three. Clean, quiet, close to how good fashion and design brands present themselves.

- Headings: Libre Caslon Display or Newsreader
- Body: Jost or Suisse style alternative such as Archivo
- Strong grid, disciplined spacing, small caps for labels and categories
- Blue used as full bands for separation, white cards sitting on top
- Gold reserved almost entirely for status and interaction

## Typography rules for all three

The current font is too heavy. Whatever we pick has to feel lighter and more elegant.

- Body text no smaller than 16px, 17 or 18 reads better
- Line length between 60 and 75 characters
- Genetics lines are long, so give them a clear hierarchy and let them break properly on mobile
- Test with real Italian text as well as English, and check that German and French navigation labels still fit

## References worth looking at

Look at how high end auction houses and studs present horses, and at how fashion and interior brands handle image galleries and product cards. The card and gallery patterns from those transfer directly.

Avoid anything that reads as a template. The whole reason they came to us is that the current site feels generic.

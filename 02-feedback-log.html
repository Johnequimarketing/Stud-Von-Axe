Feedback and audit log

Every correction, and the check that catches it next time.

Mark's feedback across the build, in his own words, turned into something checkable, and what he keeps choosing, so the next design starts closer to right. A rule that only lives in a conversation gets broken again; a rule with a check behind it does not. This page is read before starting and run through before handing anything back.

How to use it. Run node scripts/audit-homepage.mjs <file> first, which runs twenty one checks on a public page. Then walk the items tagged by eye, because a script cannot tell whether a sentence is true. Nothing goes to Mark with a red line in the audit.

What he keeps choosing

Not corrections. This is taste, read off six days of decisions, so a first draft starts closer to what he would have picked anyway. Where he has chosen the same thing twice it is written as a default; where he chose once it is written as a lean. Read this before designing, not after.

When something is on a dark ground on one page, it is on a dark ground on all of them

Read off three separate decisions in one week. The pedigree came off its navy plate because it sat under another dark block; the films went onto the navy plate because that is where the sire and dam lines already stand on a cross; and the same hero window now opens a horse page and a cross page. He does not want a house style described, he wants the same object in the same place.

Before drawing anything new, ask which existing block does this job elsewhere, and put the new thing on that block, in that position. A new plate needs a reason; matching one does not.

Taste

He wants to see it, not read that it exists

The strongest single thread of the week. Six variations rather than a recommendation, every time. om te kijken hoe dat eruit ziet, even om te checken, and then nu heb ik geen idee hoe het eruit ziet about sections that were simply absent, which is what put marked placeholders on forty three of them.

A thing that cannot be looked at cannot be judged, and an empty section is not modest, it is invisible. Build the state, mark it plainly for what it is, and let him look. Numbered variations in their own file, never a paragraph describing the options.

Taste

One publish per round, and the round is the whole message

30 Aug: push nooit bij elke kleine aanpassing, alleen als alle aanpassingen in de claude chat in een keer klaar zijn. Deploying per edit hit Vercel's hundred-a-day ceiling twice in two days, and both times the work sat finished and unseen.

Build, render, measure and audit as often as it takes; hold the sync, the deploy and the commit until everything asked for in that message is done. And when the ceiling is hit, say so in the reply rather than letting him find a stale page.

Contained and rounded, never edge to edge

Default: hold to --wrap and the gutter, round with --plate-radius, and let the plate hang or cut rather than span.

A photograph under a plate, not a photograph beside a paragraph

Default: navy plate, photograph behind it, one-colour veil, type on top. The veil is for a plate; it is never laid over the hero to make the header readable.

Never invent a value that is not in the design system

He said: nooit zomaar zwart gebruiken, dat staat nergens en hebben we nergens gebruikt, dus niet zelf met die bullshit komen. Twelve veils were written as rgba(6,12,20), a near-black that is in no token, no document and no other page.

Hard

Never shorten the copy to solve a technical problem

The gold labels on the new cards would not clear the contrast threshold, so the copy was cut to one line per card to move the text lower. He noticed immediately: ik mis de algemene tekst die er nog bij stond. The words were not the problem and should never have paid for it.

Checked: a contrast, overflow or fit problem is solved in the layout. The card grew from 4:5 to 2:3 and the dark foot was hung on the text block, and the full sentences came back unchanged. If a fix can only be bought with the client's words, it is the wrong fix.

Hard

Type on a photograph is measured behind the type, not near it

The four cards on the about page looked fine. Sampled behind the gold label itself, on the rendered page with the text hidden, the brightest card came in at 1.53:1 where the house threshold for gold on a dark ground is 3:1. Sampling a rectangle roughly where the text sits gave 2.8:1 and would have passed it.

Checked: render the state, hide the text, read the label's own bounding box out of the browser and sample that, at every width the layout changes at. Never lighten the gold to pass.

And the fix that holds: hang the dark foot on the TEXT, not on the card. A gradient measured in percentages of the card looks right on a wide screen and fails on a narrow one, because the copy wraps onto more lines while the card gets shorter, so the label rides up into the bright half. Measured at seven widths, the same card ran from 53% to 28%. A gradient on the text block, with the whole fade inside its top padding, is as tall as the words are at every width and survives the copy being rewritten. Cards now measure 3.77:1 at worst, gold, across 390 to 1440.

Hard rule

Hard rule

A form that does not send is a broken promise, sixty times over

The homepage form stops the submit and shows a thank you. Nothing is sent, nothing is stored, and the visitor is told their message arrived. Putting that on sixty more pages would have multiplied it.

The real choice, a form service or a function on Vercel, is on the client list as a decision rather than quietly made. Same rule as the Horsetelex labels and the placeholder pictures: the interface may not claim something that is not true.

The audit checks that an anchor lands on a real id, which they did, so nothing caught it. When a page is built, search for the links that were waiting for it. Fourth time this week that a label described something other than what it opens.

Hard rule

A flex rule aimed at one column, applied to both

Written as .hp__col > * it caught the plate as well as the pictures. The plate had a height forced on it and spilled its whole paragraph across the page and over the blocks below. Then the opposite: flex:0 0 auto left it at its own height beside a 460px picture on forty one of forty five pages. Both versions parsed, passed every check, and looked wrong.

Neither is visible to anything that reads markup, so there is now a third audit pass that renders all forty five horse pages in Chrome and measures them: do the columns end on the same line, is anything clipped by a container that hides its overflow, does any page scroll sideways. It skips loudly when the dev server is not up, because a check that quietly does not run is worse than no check. bash scripts/audit.sh runs all three.

Caught

The contrast check measures a list somebody typed, not the colours on the page

The accent blocks were drawn, and the audit passed without measuring them: the pairs are written by hand, so a colour nobody adds is a colour nobody checks. Worse, when the pair was added it was added as a restatement of the CSS, and the two immediately disagreed: the label was gold on gold in the stylesheet while the audit measured navy at sixty two percent, and reported a figure belonging to neither.

The two new pairs read their percentages out of the stylesheet with a regex instead of restating them, and skip honestly when they cannot be found rather than falling back to a guess. The label then measured 4.42:1, under the floor, and went to seventy two percent. Proven by putting sixty two back. The list is still written by hand: every new coloured surface has to be added to it on the day it is drawn, and that is now said in a comment above the list.

Caught

The audit runs after every change, and it checks more than tokens

30 Aug: Na elke change, kun je dan ook een audit draaien? of alle layout klopt, consistent is? alle informatie klopt, alle teksten kloppen, alle formuleringen kloppen, geen gekke woorden, of alles wel equestrian minded is. alle lettertypes, lettergroottes, kleuren etc.

One command, bash scripts/audit.sh, in two passes. Every page against the rules, and then the pages against each other and against horses-data.js, which is the half that could not be checked one page at a time. What it added that day: the type scale, written down in index.html rather than inferred, so adding a size is a decision; every border-radius a token, a circle or a pill; the words a horse person would not write; every figure on a page traceable to the record and no figure on a page the record does not have; one spelling per horse across its page and its card; one set of layout tokens across all of them.

Caught

Three reading sizes for one job, and a component defined twice

The first run of the new checks found them straight away. Running copy was set at 16, 17 and 17.5 pixels in three different files; the pedigree cell had a hand written 10px corner that is exactly --ctl-radius; and the country flag was defined twice, 18 by 12 in one block and 16 by 11 in another, with the browser silently using the second and the first sitting there as dead code that still read like a decision.

One reading size, the token where a token exists, one definition per component. None of this is visible in a render, which is why it needs a check rather than an eye.

Caught

A date field that is sometimes a date, in two shapes

Sixteen of the twenty one foals carry a full date of birth where the others carry a year, and in two formats: 07/05/25 beside 28/03/2025. Printed raw, one card read "Born 2026" and the next "Born 07/05/25".

The order is day/month/year, and that is read off the data rather than assumed: eight of the sixteen have a first number above twelve, none has a second one above twelve, and every month falls between March and June, which is the foaling season. A card shows the year, where it stands beside other cards; the page writes the date out in full, "7 May 2025".

Hard rule

A control with nothing behind it: arrows, dots or a play button and no code that hears them

Twice in one hour. Once an edit next to the media script took the whole script out with it, and once the condition that attaches it tested for class="hgal" while the page said class="hgal is-stand". Neither is a parse error, neither changes what the page looks like, and the audit passed both times: the buttons were simply dead.

The audit now checks that every data hook a control carries is read by a script on the same page: data-yt, data-full, data-step, data-to. Proven on 30 Aug against both faults, one at a time. A rendered page and a green parse are not proof that anything works.

Caught

A comment is still text in the output, and the closing script tag ends a script from inside one

The contact form broke all sixty pages at once. The comment above it warned about the closing script tag by naming it, and naming it is what ended the script: the browser reads the characters, not the intent. The line under it, joining the message with a line break, had the same shape of bug, a single backslash-n that the template literal turned into a real line break inside a string. Neither showed on the page: a browser that hits a syntax error stops running the block and renders everything else exactly as before.

The audit now parses every inline block on every page with new Function, and both bugs were put back one at a time to watch it fail. Proven on 30 Aug. It catches a script that cannot parse; it does not catch one that parses and then throws, which is what the slider's at() did.

Caught

Three behaviours in one closure: the slider's at() and the lightbox's at counter

The arrows did nothing at all. The console said "at is not a function": the film slider declared var at = function() and the picture viewer, further down the same IIFE, declared var at = 0. Hoisting turned the function into a number before either ran.

One closure per behaviour, so nothing in one can see anything in the other. And a build whose output is thrown away with >/dev/null hides the very failure you are about to render: watch the build, then render.

Caught

scrollTo({behavior:'smooth'}) is silently ignored by some engines

With the naming fixed the arrows still did nothing, in silence: no error, no movement. Setting scrollLeft directly worked; the same call with smooth behaviour did not.

Set the value in the script and let scroll-behavior in the stylesheet decide whether that is a glide or a jump. It is honoured or harmlessly skipped, and the reduced-motion rule already turns it off. The dots and arrows are painted from the index being moved to rather than from a scroll event, which does not fire everywhere either.

Caught

Every one of these cost time in this build. They are listed so the check is obvious rather than the lesson being memorable.

Before handing anything back

A new check is proven against the bug it was written for, by reintroducing that bug and watching the check fail, before it is trusted.

The audit runs green on the file that changed, and on every page that inherits from it. A change to index.html is a change to six pages: rebuild them (node scripts/build-news.mjs and node scripts/build-about.mjs) and audit them all, or the site is consistent only in the one file that was looked at.

The browser reports no console errors and no sideways scroll.

The change is measured in the browser, not judged from the source.

Every new claim in the copy has a named source.

Rendered at 1440 and at 390, and both were actually looked at.

Open questions for the client are listed, not silently decided.

Publishing: since 28 Aug he has asked for the work to go live, so the reply states what was pushed, what is live and on which URL, with the status codes checked. Before that date the rule was the opposite, and outside what he has asked for it still is: nothing is committed, pushed or published on our own judgement.

Kept alongside the design system and the build checklist. When new feedback arrives it is added here first, and only then acted on.
